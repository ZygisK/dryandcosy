Old wooden floors look lovely and leak a surprising amount of cold air through the gaps between boards and under the skirting. You do not need to lift the floor to fix most of it.

## 1. Fill the gaps between boards

A flexible floorboard gap filler pushes into the gaps and seals them while letting the wood move. It is nearly invisible once in and can be pulled out later, which makes it fine for renters.

[[product id="floor-gap"]]

## 2. Seal under the skirting

Where floor meets wall, cold air often streams in. A self-adhesive seal strip or a thin bead of filler along the bottom of the skirting stops it.

[[product id="seal-strip"]]

## 3. Rugs on hard floors

A big rug with a thick underlay is the easiest win of all. Warmer feet, warmer room, and it looks cosy.

## 4. Stop the door draughts too

Floor draughts are often worst near doors. A brush strip or a draught excluder at the bottom of each door finishes the job.

[[product id="door-brush"]]

[[product id="draught-excluder"]]

## How to find floor draughts

On a cold, windy day, hold the back of your hand or a lit incense stick near the floor and skirting. Where it flickers or feels cold, there is a gap.

## Do not block air bricks

Suspended timber floors need air underneath to stay dry. Seal the gaps in the room, but never block the air bricks on the outside walls.
