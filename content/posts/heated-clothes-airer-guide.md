Yes, for most homes without a tumble dryer a heated airer is worth it, as long as you buy one with a cover or add one. Uncovered, it warms the room more than the clothes. Covered, it dries a full load in a few hours for pennies.

## What a heated airer actually does

A heated airer is a clothes rack with low-wattage heating elements inside the bars. The bars get warm, not hot, so they are safe for most fabrics. On their own they speed drying a little. The real gain comes from trapping that warmth around the clothes with a fitted cover, which turns the airer into a small drying cabinet.

## How much it costs to run

Most heated airers use between 150 and 300 watts. At typical UK and Irish electricity prices that works out at roughly 5 to 10p (or cents) an hour.

| Setup | Typical time for a full load | Rough cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal airer, unheated room | 1 to 3 days | Free, but damp walls |
| Heated airer, no cover | 8 to 12 hours | 50p to £1 |
| Heated airer with cover | 3 to 6 hours | 20p to 50p |
| Tumble dryer (condenser) | 1 to 2 hours | £1 or more |

The cheapest dry is still outdoors on a breezy day. On grey, wet days, a covered heated airer is the cheapest way to get clothes properly dry indoors.

## Do you need a dehumidifier as well?

A heated airer dries clothes faster, but the water still goes into the room. In a small flat or a home with condensation problems, pair it with a dehumidifier in the same room and keep the door shut. The two together dry a load quickly and leave the windows dry.

## Tips to dry faster

1. Spin the wash on the highest setting the fabric allows.
2. Leave a hand's width between items. Crowded clothes dry slowly.
3. Put heavy items like jeans and towels on the top rail, where warm air collects.
4. Turn items halfway through.
5. Run it in the smallest room you can, door shut, with a dehumidifier if you have one.

## Common questions

**Are heated airers safe to leave on overnight?**
Most are designed for long use at low temperature, but follow the manufacturer's instructions and never cover one with anything other than its own fitted cover.

**Can you put towels on a heated airer?**
Yes. Spread them out rather than folding them over a bar, and turn them halfway through.

**Is a heated airer cheaper than a tumble dryer?**
Per load, usually yes. It is slower, so a tumble dryer still wins if you need clothes in an hour.
