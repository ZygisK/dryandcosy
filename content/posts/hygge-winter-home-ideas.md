Hygge is the Danish idea of cosiness: warm light, soft textures, good company and nowhere to be. It happens to be one of the cheapest ways to make a home feel warmer in winter, because most of it is about how a room feels, not how hot it is.

## 1. Lighting is everything

Turn off the big overhead light. Use lamps at different heights, warm white bulbs and candles or LED candles. A room lit low and warm feels a few degrees warmer.

[[product id="led-bulbs"]]

## 2. Layers of soft texture

A heavy knit throw on the sofa, a sheepskin on a chair, cushions you can sink into. A heated throw gives you the look and the warmth.

[[product id="heated-throw"]]

## 3. A warm, heavy bed

A wool duvet and an electric blanket switched on before bed make the bedroom the cosiest room in the house.

[[product id="wool-duvet"]]

## 4. Close the curtains early

Drawing thick curtains at dusk makes a room feel enclosed and safe, and keeps heat in.

[[product id="thermal-curtains"]]

## 5. Small warm rituals

- A hot water bottle on your lap with a film.
- A pot of tea or hot chocolate.
- Socks, slippers and a soft jumper instead of turning the heating up.
- A book and a candle, phones away.

[[product id="hot-water-bottle"]]

## Hygge on a budget checklist

- Lamps, not the big light
- Warm white bulbs
- One heavy throw per sofa
- Thermal curtains closed at dusk
- Wool duvet or electric blanket
- Hot water bottle with a soft cover
