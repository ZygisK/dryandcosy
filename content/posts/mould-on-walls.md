Mould on walls is almost always condensation: warm, damp air hitting a cold wall, usually an outside wall, behind furniture or in a corner. Clean it, repaint with anti-mould paint, and bring the humidity down, and it stops coming back.

## Step 1: Clean it safely

Open a window, wear gloves and a mask, and use a mould and mildew spray. Leave it for the time on the label, wipe with a cloth you throw away, and repeat if needed. Do not dry-brush mould, which spreads the spores.

[[product id="mould-spray"]]

## Step 2: Repaint with anti-mould paint

Once the wall is clean and completely dry, anti-mould paint stops regrowth on the surface. Ordinary emulsion over mould just hides it for a few weeks.

[[product id="anti-mould-paint"]]

## Step 3: Bring the humidity down

Mould needs damp. Keep indoor humidity between 40 and 60%. A hygrometer tells you where you are.

[[product id="hygrometer"]]

For a damp house, a 20 litre dehumidifier is the fix that makes the biggest difference, and it dries washing too.

[[product id="dehumidifier-meaco-20l"]]

## Step 4: Let air reach the cold walls

- Pull wardrobes, beds and sofas 5 to 10cm off outside walls.
- Keep trickle vents open and open windows briefly each morning.
- Keep a low, steady background heat in cold rooms.
- Never dry washing in a room with mould.

## When it is not condensation

A damp patch with a tide mark, mould low on the wall near the floor, or damp that appears after rain can mean rising damp, a leak or a gutter problem. That needs a builder or, if you rent, your landlord.
