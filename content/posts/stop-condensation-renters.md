Renting means you cannot fit new windows or an extractor fan, but you can still stop most condensation. The trick is to cut moisture at the source and clear what forms before it soaks into frames and sills.

## 1. Clear the water every morning

Condensation that sits all day soaks into seals and frames and turns black. A window vacuum clears a whole flat in a couple of minutes and the water goes down the sink, not onto the sill.

[[product id="window-vac"]]

## 2. Open the trickle vents

Most modern windows have a thin vent strip along the top. Slide it open and leave it open all winter. It lets moist air out without a draught you will notice.

## 3. Know your humidity

A cheap hygrometer tells you whether the air is the problem. Aim for 40 to 60%. If it sits above 60% most mornings, the next step is a dehumidifier.

[[product id="hygrometer"]]

## 4. A small dehumidifier in the worst room

Usually the bedroom. A compact dehumidifier runs quietly overnight and is the single biggest fix for wet windows. It comes with you when you move.

[[product id="dehumidifier-quiet"]]

[[product id="dehumidifier-mini"]]

## 5. Moisture traps for cupboards and sills

Disposable moisture trap tubs are not a fix for a whole room, but they help on windowsills, in wardrobes and under the sink.

[[product id="moisture-trap"]]

## 6. Daily habits that make the biggest difference

- Lids on pans when cooking, and the cooker hood on.
- Dry washing in one room with the door shut and a window cracked.
- Bedroom doors open at night so moisture spreads out.
- Furniture a few centimetres off outside walls.

## 7. Thermal curtains, but open them in the day

Thermal curtains keep heat in at night. Open them fully in the morning so the glass and frame can dry out.

[[product id="thermal-curtains"]]

## Is it your landlord's job?

Persistent damp caused by leaks, broken extractor fans or failed seals is usually the landlord's responsibility. Condensation from everyday living is mostly about ventilation and habits. Photograph any mould and report it in writing if it keeps coming back.
