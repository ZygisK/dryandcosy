The cheapest heat is the heat you do not need. Warm yourself first, stop warm air leaking out second, and only then heat the rooms you actually use. Done in that order, most homes can keep the thermostat lower and still feel cosier.

## Step 1: Heat yourself, not the house

Heating a whole house to warm one person on the sofa is the expensive way. Personal heat costs pennies.

| What | Typical power | Rough cost per hour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heated throw | 100 to 150W | 3 to 5p |
| Electric blanket (pre-heat bed) | 60 to 120W | 2 to 4p |
| Heated foot warmer | 50 to 100W | 2 to 3p |
| Oil radiator, one room | 1,000 to 2,000W | 30 to 60p |
| Gas central heating, whole house | varies | far more |

## Step 2: Keep the heat you pay for

Draughts can lose a surprising share of your heat. These fixes cost a few pounds each and most work in rentals.

- A brush strip on the bottom of every external door.
- A letterbox brush and a keyhole cover.
- A chimney draught stopper in any fireplace you do not use.
- Thermal curtains, closed as soon as it gets dark.
- Reflector panels behind radiators on outside walls.

## Step 3: Heat only the rooms you use

## Step 4: Keep the air dry

Damp air feels colder than dry air at the same temperature, and it costs more to heat. If your windows stream every morning, sorting the moisture makes the house feel warmer too. See [the ultimate guide to a dry, warm home](/ultimate-guide-dry-warm-home/).

## A warm evening routine

1. Close curtains at dusk.
2. Draught excluder against the living room door.
3. Heated throw on, thermostat down a degree or two.
4. Electric blanket on 30 minutes before bed, then off.

## Common questions

**What is the cheapest way to stay warm at home?**
Personal heat: a heated throw, electric blanket or hot water bottle, plus warm layers. They cost a few pence an hour.

**Is it cheaper to leave the heating on low all day?**
Usually not. For most homes it is cheaper to heat when you need it, with good draught proofing to hold the heat in.

**Do radiator reflector panels work?**
On radiators fixed to outside walls, yes. They bounce heat back into the room instead of into the wall.
