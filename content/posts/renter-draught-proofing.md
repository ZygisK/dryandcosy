Renting a draughty house is miserable, but you do not need your landlord's permission or a drill to fix most of it. Everything here sticks on, slides in or sits in place, and comes off cleanly when you move out.

## The renter friendly fix list

| Draught | Fix | Comes off cleanly? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under the front door | Draught excluder or stick-on brush | Yes |
| Letterbox | Internal letterbox brush, clips or sticks on | Yes |
| Keyhole | Stick-on keyhole cover | Yes |
| Door and window frames | Self-adhesive foam seal | Yes, peels off |
| Single glazed windows | Window insulation film | Yes, peels off |
| Unused fireplace | Chimney balloon | Yes, deflate and pull out |
| Floorboard gaps | Flexible gap filler | Yes, pulls out |
| Front door opens into the room | Thermal door curtain on a tension rod | Yes |

## Doors

A heavy draught excluder needs nothing fitted at all. If you want a brush strip, choose a self-adhesive one.

## Fireplaces

An unused fireplace can be the biggest draught in the room. A chimney balloon inflates inside the flue and deflates when you want it out. Leave a note on the fireplace so nobody lights a fire with it in.

## Floors

Flexible gap filler presses into the gaps between boards and pulls out when you leave. A big rug does the rest.

[[product id="floor-gap"]]

## Tips for your deposit

- Photograph everything before you start and after you remove it.
- Test any adhesive on a hidden spot first.
- Use the lowest-tack seal strips on painted frames.
- Never block air bricks, trickle vents or boiler ventilation.

## Should the landlord fix it?

Broken seals, rotten frames and doors that do not close properly are usually the landlord's job. Report them in writing. The fixes above are for the everyday draughts that make a home cold in the meantime.
