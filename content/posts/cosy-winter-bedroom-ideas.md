A cosy winter bedroom is mostly about the bed, not the radiator. Warm the bed, stop the cold coming off the window, keep the air dry, and soften the light. You can sleep warmer with the bedroom heating turned down.

## 1. Warm the bed before you get in

An electric blanket switched on 30 minutes before bed and off when you get in costs a couple of pence a night. A dual-control one lets each side choose their own heat.

[[product id="heated-underblanket-dual"]]

## 2. Swap to a wool duvet

Wool keeps you warm without the clammy, overheated feeling of a heavy synthetic duvet, because it handles moisture better. It is the single biggest comfort upgrade for winter.

[[product id="wool-duvet"]]

## 3. Layer a heavy throw

A thick knit or faux fur throw across the foot of the bed adds warmth where you need it and does most of the visual work in making a room look cosy.

[[product id="heated-throw"]]

## 4. Thermal curtains, closed at dusk

Bedroom windows lose a lot of heat at night. Lined thermal curtains, pulled as soon as it gets dark, keep the cold glass out of the room and help with early light in the morning too.

[[product id="thermal-curtains"]]

## 5. Soft, warm light

Turn the big light off. A lamp on each side, warm white bulbs, and maybe a string of fairy lights. Low, warm light makes a room feel warmer than it is.

[[product id="led-bulbs"]]

## 6. Keep the air dry

Bedrooms get the worst condensation because two people breathe out a lot of moisture overnight. Damp air feels colder and leads to mould behind wardrobes. A quiet dehumidifier fixes it.

[[product id="dehumidifier-quiet"]]

## 7. Stop the draught under the door

A simple door draught excluder keeps warm air in and cold hallway air out.

[[product id="draught-excluder"]]

## Quick checklist

- Electric blanket on 30 minutes before bed
- Wool duvet and a heavy throw
- Thermal curtains closed at dusk
- Lamps, not the big light
- Humidity under 60%
- Draught excluder at the door
