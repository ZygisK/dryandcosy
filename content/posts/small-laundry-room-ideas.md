Most UK and Irish homes do not have a utility room. The washing machine lives in the kitchen and the drying happens wherever there is space. A few smart choices turn that chaos into a small, tidy laundry corner.

## 1. Pick one drying spot and kit it out

Decide where washing dries, ideally a small room with a window or the bathroom, and keep everything there. Drying in one place with the door shut keeps damp out of the rest of the house.

## 2. Go vertical with your airer

A tall tiered airer holds a full load in a tiny footprint and folds flat behind a door.

[[product id="tiered-airer"]]

## 3. Use the walls and the back of the door

A fold-down wall rack and an over-door rack give you drying space that disappears when you are done.

[[product id="wall-airer"]]

[[product id="overdoor-airer"]]

## 4. Spin out more water first

A small spin dryer removes far more water than a washing machine's spin, so clothes dry much faster on the airer and put less moisture into the air. It fits in a cupboard.

[[product id="spin-dryer"]]

## 5. A drying pod for wet weeks

A heated drying pod is a zip-up wardrobe with warm air blown through it. Good for shirts and work clothes when it rains all week, and it folds away.

[[product id="drying-pod"]]

## 6. Keep the damp in check

A small laundry space gets humid fast. A compressor dehumidifier with a laundry mode dries clothes quicker and protects walls and windows.

[[product id="dehumidifier-compressor"]]

## Organisation ideas

- Baskets stacked by colour or person, not one big pile.
- A shelf above the machine for detergent and pegs.
- Hooks on the side of a cupboard for the ironing board and airer.
- A slim trolley between the machine and the wall.
