Short answer: an electric blanket for the bed, a heated throw for the sofa. They do different jobs, and both cost a few pence an hour, far less than heating a room.

## At a glance

| | Electric blanket | Heated throw | Hot water bottle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | Warming the bed | Sitting on the sofa | Quick, free-ish warmth |
| Typical power | 60 to 120W | 100 to 150W | None |
| Rough cost per hour | 2 to 4p | 3 to 5p | Cost of boiling a kettle |
| Use | Pre-heat, then off or low | All evening | An hour or two |

## Electric blanket

An electric under-blanket sits under your sheet and warms the mattress. Switch it on half an hour before bed and the whole bed is warm when you get in, so the bedroom heating can stay low or off. Look for multiple heat settings, an auto shut-off timer and machine-washable fabric. If you share a bed, get dual controls.

## Heated throw

A heated throw is a soft blanket with fine heating wires, used over you on the sofa. It is the cheapest way to stay warm in the evening, because you heat one person instead of a whole room. Choose one with a timer and a washable fabric.

## Hot water bottle

Still a great, cheap option for short spells. A soft cover keeps it comfortable and safer. Replace it every couple of years, as the rubber ages.

## Which should you buy first?

- **You feel coldest in bed:** electric blanket.
- **You feel coldest in the evening on the sofa:** heated throw.
- **You work from home:** heated throw, plus a heated foot warmer under the desk.
- **Tight budget:** hot water bottle now, then add the others.

## Safety basics

- Never use an electric blanket and a hot water bottle together.
- Do not fold or tuck in a heated throw while it is switched on, follow the maker's guidance.
- Check cables and fabric for wear each winter, and replace anything damaged.
