Washing smells damp because it dried too slowly. When clothes stay wet for more than about a day, bacteria grow in the fabric and leave that musty smell. Speed up drying and keep the machine clean, and the smell goes.

## The 6 usual causes

| Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Dried in a cold, closed room | Warmest room, door shut, dehumidifier on |
| Hung too close together | Space it out on a tall airer |
| Not spun hard enough | Highest spin, or a spin dryer |
| Left sitting in the drum | Hang it the moment the cycle ends |
| Took more than a day to dry | Heated airer with a cover |
| The machine itself smells | Hot empty wash once a month |

## 1. Get more water out first

The less water in the clothes, the faster they dry. Use the highest spin the fabric allows. A small spin dryer pulls out far more water in a few minutes.

[[product id="spin-dryer"]]

## 2. Give clothes room

A crowded airer traps damp air between items. A tall tiered airer lets you space everything out in a small footprint.

[[product id="tiered-airer"]]

## 3. Dry it in hours, not days

A heated airer with a cover gets a full load dry in a few hours, well before the smell can start.

[[product id="airer-cover-bundle"]]

## 4. Take the moisture out of the room

Drying in a small, closed room fills the air with water, which slows everything down. A dehumidifier with a laundry mode dries clothes overnight and keeps windows and walls dry too.

[[product id="dehumidifier-compressor"]]

## 5. Clean the washing machine

If even freshly washed clothes smell, the machine is the problem. Run an empty wash at 60 or 90 degrees once a month, wipe the door seal, and leave the door and drawer open between washes.

## Getting the smell out of clothes that already smell

Rewash warm with a little less detergent than usual and a cup of white vinegar in the softener drawer, then dry them fast using the steps above. Skip fabric softener, which coats fibres and traps smells.
