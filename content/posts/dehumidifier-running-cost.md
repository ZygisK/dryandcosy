Most compressor dehumidifiers cost a few pence an hour to run, roughly the same as a fridge or a couple of light bulbs. Desiccant models cost more per hour but work better in cold rooms. For most homes the cost is small next to what damp, mould and wet washing would cost you.

## Typical running costs

| Type | Typical power | Rough cost per hour | 8 hours a day for a month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mini dehumidifier | 20 to 30W | under 1p | well under £3 |
| Quiet 10 to 12L compressor | 150 to 200W | 4 to 6p | £10 to £15 |
| 20L compressor | 250 to 400W | 7 to 12p | £17 to £30 |
| Desiccant | 300 to 650W | 8 to 18p | £20 to £45 |

Costs are approximate and depend on your electricity rate. Most units also cycle off once the room reaches the target humidity, so real costs are usually lower.

## Compressor: cheapest in a heated home

Compressor dehumidifiers are the most efficient choice when the room is above about 15°C. A quiet 10L model is enough for a bedroom or flat.

A bigger unit removes water faster, so it reaches your target humidity and switches off sooner. With laundry mode, it replaces a lot of tumble dryer use.

## Desiccant: costs more, works in the cold

Desiccant models use more power, but they keep working in cold, unheated rooms where a compressor slows right down. They also give off a little warmth.

## How to keep the bill down

1. Set a target of 50 to 55%, not as low as it will go.
2. Shut the door of the room you are drying.
3. Use laundry mode only when washing is drying.
4. Run it on a timer overnight or while you are out.
5. Clean the filter every few weeks.

## Is it worth it?

Usually yes. A dehumidifier dries washing, stops condensation and keeps mould away for less than the cost of running a tumble dryer a few times a week.
