All plug-in electric heaters turn electricity into heat at about the same efficiency, so the cost per hour mostly comes down to wattage and how long it runs. The real difference is how they heat: fast and focused, or slow and steady.

## Side by side

| | Oil filled radiator | Ceramic fan heater |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical power | 1,000 to 2,500W | 1,000 to 2,000W |
| Rough cost per hour at full power | 30 to 70p | 30 to 60p |
| Heats up | Slowly, stays warm after it switches off | In minutes |
| Best for | A room you sit in for hours | Quick bursts, small rooms, a home office |
| Noise | Silent | Fan noise |

Costs vary with your electricity rate. A thermostat that cycles the heater on and off cuts the real cost a lot.

## Oil filled radiator

Oil filled radiators warm the oil inside, which keeps radiating heat after the element switches off. Use a lower setting with the thermostat and it holds a room at a steady temperature for less than you would expect.

## Ceramic heater with a thermostat

A ceramic fan heater warms a small room in minutes. Ideal for a quick blast in a bathroom or home office. Choose one with a thermostat, not just high and low.

[[product id="ceramic-heater"]]

## Cut the cost of any heater

1. Use the thermostat, not full power all evening.
2. Shut the door and heat the smallest room you can.
3. Put it on a timer plug so it is never left on.
4. Draught-proof the room first.
