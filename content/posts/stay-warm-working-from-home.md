Working from home in winter can quietly double your heating bill, because the whole house is warm all day for one person at a desk. Heat yourself and one small space instead, and you can keep the thermostat low until the evening.

## 1. Warm feet first

Cold feet make your whole body feel cold. A heated foot warmer under the desk uses very little power and makes a huge difference.

[[product id="foot-warmer"]]

## 2. A heated gilet

A rechargeable heated gilet keeps your core warm while you type and moves with you around the house. It is the most useful winter gadget for home workers.

[[product id="heated-gilet"]]

## 3. A heated throw over your lap

For long calls or slow afternoons, a heated throw over your legs costs a few pence an hour.

[[product id="heated-throw"]]

## 4. A small heater for one room, on a timer

If the room itself is cold, a ceramic heater with a thermostat warms a small office quickly. Use it in short bursts and plug it into a timer.

[[product id="ceramic-heater"]]

[[product id="timer-plug"]]

## 5. Shut the door and stop the draught

Work in the smallest room you can, door shut, with a draught excluder at the bottom.

[[product id="draught-excluder"]]

## Rough costs per hour

| What | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| Foot warmer | 1 to 3p |
| Heated throw | 3 to 5p |
| Heated gilet | A few pence per charge |
| Ceramic heater, small room | 20 to 60p |
| Whole house central heating | Much more |
