Most winter misery at home comes from two things: too much moisture in the air, and warm air leaking out. Fix those and the wet windows, musty smells, black mould and high heating bills mostly go away together. This guide puts everything in one place. Save it and work through it a section at a time.

## The 60 second version

1. Keep indoor humidity between 40 and 60%. A cheap hygrometer tells you where you are.
2. Dry washing in one room with the door shut and a window cracked, or with a dehumidifier running.
3. Wipe or vacuum window condensation every morning before it soaks into frames.
4. Kill existing mould, then stop the moisture that feeds it.
5. Block draughts at doors, letterboxes and chimneys before you turn the heating up.
6. Heat yourself, not the whole house, in the evenings.

## 1. Drying washing indoors without the damp

One load of washing releases around two litres of water into your home. If it dries on radiators around the house, that water ends up on your windows and walls.

- Spin on the highest setting the fabric allows. Less water in means less water in the air.
- Pick one room, shut the door and crack a window, or run a dehumidifier on laundry mode.
- Space clothes out. A full, crowded airer can take two or three days.
- On grey days, a heated airer with a cover dries a full load in a few hours for pennies.

## 2. Stopping condensation on windows

Wet windows in the morning are a moisture problem in the room, not a window problem. Overnight, breath and warm air hit cold glass.

- Open trickle vents, or crack the window for ten minutes after you wake.
- Clear the water every morning. A window vacuum does a whole house in minutes.
- Keep bedroom doors open at night so moisture spreads out instead of concentrating.
- If it is bad every day, a quiet bedroom dehumidifier fixes it.

## 3. Black mould: clean it, then stop it

Mould comes back because cleaning removes the mould but not the moisture. Do both.

- Clean spots with a proper mould remover, gloves on and window open.
- In bathrooms, squeegee the tiles after every shower and run the fan for 15 minutes.
- Replace blackened silicone with anti-mould sealant rather than cleaning it forever.
- On walls and ceilings, repaint with anti-mould paint once the area is clean and dry.

## 5. Draughts: keep the heat you pay for

Before turning the thermostat up, stop warm air escaping. These fixes cost a few pounds each and work in rentals.

- A brush strip along the bottom of the front door.
- A letterbox brush and keyhole cover.
- A chimney blocker in any fireplace you do not use.
- Thermal curtains, closed at dusk.

## 6. Staying warm for less

Heating the whole house for one person on the sofa is the expensive way. Heat the person instead.

- A heated throw on the sofa costs pennies an hour.
- An electric blanket warms the bed so the bedroom heating can stay low.
- Smart radiator valves heat only the rooms you use.

## Common questions

**What humidity should my house be?**
Between 40 and 60%. Above 60% for long stretches is when condensation and mould take hold.

**Is it bad to dry clothes on radiators?**
It is not dangerous, but it pushes humidity up and stops the radiator heating the room properly. An airer near the radiator works almost as well.

**What is the single best fix for a damp home?**
For most homes, a dehumidifier sized to the house, plus drying washing in one room.
