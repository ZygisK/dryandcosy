If your bathroom has a working extractor fan and a window, you probably do not need a dehumidifier. If it has no fan, a weak one, or mould keeps coming back on the ceiling, a compact dehumidifier is the easiest fix.

## Signs your bathroom needs help

- Mirror and tiles still wet 30 minutes after a shower.
- Black spots on the ceiling, silicone or grout that return after cleaning.
- Paint peeling or bubbling.
- A musty smell in towels.

## Fix the fan first, if you can

A fan with a humidity sensor turns itself on when the room is steamy and runs until it is dry. If you own your home, this is the best long-term fix.

## Which dehumidifier for a bathroom?

Bathrooms are small and often cool, and they are wet, so look for a compact unit that you can place outside the splash zone or just outside the door.

**Compact bathroom dehumidifier.** Small, light and quiet. Run it for an hour or two after showers.

[[product id="bath-dehumidifier"]]

**Desiccant dehumidifier.** Better than a compressor model in a cold bathroom, because it keeps working at low temperatures.

[[product id="dehumidifier-desiccant"]]

**Mini dehumidifier.** Fine for a tiny en-suite with a light problem. Empty it often.

[[product id="dehumidifier-mini"]]

## Where to put it

Never where it can be splashed. On the landing just outside the bathroom with the door open after a shower works well, or on a shelf away from the bath and basin. Always plug it into a normal socket outside the bathroom.

## Pair it with a two minute routine

1. Squeegee tiles and screen after every shower.
2. Door shut, window cracked, dehumidifier or fan on for 30 minutes.
3. Towels dried outside the bathroom.
