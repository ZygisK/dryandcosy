Towels are the hardest thing to dry indoors. They are thick, they hold a lot of water, and if they take more than a day they start to smell. Get as much water out as possible, give them space and warm air, and they will be dry and fresh by evening.

## 1. Spin hard

Use the highest spin your machine allows for towels. Better still, give them a few minutes in a spin dryer, which pulls out far more water.

[[product id="spin-dryer"]]

## 2. Shake them out

A good shake before hanging fluffs the fibres, so they dry faster and feel softer.

## 3. Hang them open, not folded

Folding a towel over a rail doubles its thickness and doubles the drying time. Spread it flat on a wing airer, or over two rails with a gap.

[[product id="heated-airer-wing"]]

## 4. Warm air, not a radiator

Draping towels on radiators makes them stiff and fills the room with moisture. A heated airer with a cover, or a heated drying pod, dries them evenly.

[[product id="drying-pod"]]

## 5. Take the moisture out of the room

A dehumidifier on laundry mode in the same room, door shut, dries towels overnight and stops the musty smell before it starts.

[[product id="dehumidifier-compressor"]]

## Why do towels smell damp?

Slow drying. If towels stay damp for more than a day, bacteria grow and leave that musty smell. Fix the drying, and wash smelly towels hot with half the usual detergent and no fabric softener, which coats the fibres.

## Quick routine

1. Highest spin, or spin dryer.
2. Shake out.
3. Hang open on a wing or heated airer.
4. Dehumidifier on, door shut.
5. Flip halfway through.
