The front door is often the single biggest draught in a house. Cold air comes in underneath it, around the frame, through the letterbox and even through the keyhole. All of it can be fixed in an afternoon for the price of a takeaway, and almost all of it works in a rental.

## 1. A brush strip along the bottom

The gap under the door is usually the worst. A brush strip screws or sticks to the bottom of the door and seals the gap without dragging on the floor.

[[product id="door-brush"]]

## 2. A draught excluder on the inside

If you cannot fit a strip, a heavy draught excluder along the bottom of the door does the job and moves with you.

[[product id="draught-excluder"]]

## 3. Seal the frame

Self-adhesive foam or rubber seal strips around the frame stop the whistling and the cold edges. Press the door closed on a piece of paper: if it slides out easily, that side needs a seal.

[[product id="seal-strip"]]

## 4. A letterbox brush

Letterboxes leak a surprising amount of heat. An internal letterbox brush still lets the post through.

[[product id="letterbox"]]

## 5. A keyhole cover

Tiny, cheap, and it stops a pinpoint of cold air you can feel on a windy night.

[[product id="keyhole-cover"]]

## 6. A thermal door curtain

For older doors or homes where the front door opens straight into the living room, a heavy thermal curtain on a rail is the most effective fix of all.

[[product id="door-curtain"]]

## How to find the draughts

On a windy day, run a damp hand or a lit incense stick slowly around the door. Where the smoke moves or your hand feels cold, there is a gap.
