A hot water bottle is still the cheapest way to stay warm: one kettle of water gives hours of heat. A good one with a soft cover, used safely, beats turning the heating up for an evening on the sofa.

## What to look for

- **A soft cover.** Knitted or fleece covers protect your skin and hold the heat longer.
- **A good stopper** that seals tightly.
- **A date marker.** Most bottles have a flower-shaped date mark near the neck showing when they were made.
- **Size.** Standard 2 litre for the bed or sofa, smaller ones for hands or a lap.

## How to fill it safely

1. Use hot water from a kettle that has cooled for a few minutes, never boiling.
2. Fill it about two thirds full.
3. Hold it upright and gently squeeze out the air before closing.
4. Screw the stopper in firmly and check for leaks.
5. Always use it in its cover.

## When to replace it

Rubber ages. Replace your hot water bottle every couple of years, or straight away if it looks cracked, perished or swollen.

## Never mix heat sources

Do not use a hot water bottle in a bed with an electric blanket switched on.

## Cosy extras for less

For the bed, an electric blanket warms it in half an hour for a couple of pence. For long evenings on the sofa, a heated throw keeps the heat steady.
