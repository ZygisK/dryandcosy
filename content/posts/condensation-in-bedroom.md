Bedrooms get the worst condensation in the house. Two people breathe out a lot of moisture overnight, the door is usually shut, and the room is often cooler than the rest of the house. By morning, that moisture is running down the window.

## Why it happens

- Breathing and sweating add moisture all night.
- Doors shut, so the damp air has nowhere to go.
- Cool bedrooms mean cold glass and cold outside walls.
- Washing dried in the bedroom makes it much worse.

## 1. Measure it

A hygrometer by the bed tells you what is going on. Over 60% humidity most mornings means condensation and mould are coming.

[[product id="hygrometer"]]

## 2. A quiet dehumidifier overnight

The single biggest fix. A quiet compressor model runs through the night without keeping you awake and the windows are dry in the morning.

[[product id="dehumidifier-quiet"]]

## 3. Clear the glass every morning

A window vacuum takes the water off before it soaks into frames and seals.

[[product id="window-vac"]]

## 4. Let air move

- Open trickle vents and leave them open.
- Leave the bedroom door ajar at night.
- Crack the window for ten minutes after you get up.
- Pull wardrobes and beds a little away from outside walls.

## 5. Keep the room evenly warm

A very cold bedroom gets more condensation. A low, steady background temperature helps, and thermal curtains keep the glass from getting as cold.

[[product id="thermal-curtains"]]

## Never dry washing in the bedroom

One load releases litres of water. If you have to, run the dehumidifier on laundry mode with the door shut and sleep elsewhere until it is dry.
